Why the words take longer than 474 ms to appear
  • Update cadence: inference starts every 1 second, adding roughly 0–1 second of phase wait during ongoing speech.
  • Starting a session: the initial collection window is 2 seconds. Recent local replay tests delivered the first caption at about 2.54–2.56 seconds.
  • Your laptop: camera/encoding, tailnet transport and display time are outside these server logs. The app’s Update timing panel measures capture-to-display age and connection round trip.
Speech starts, pauses and finalization
  • The speaking detector needs 480 ms of future context and uses a 1-second rolling mean. Starting speech requires three consecutive means above the start threshold.
  • A sentence closes after 800 ms of confirmed quiet in the smoothed evidence. Context, smoothing, scheduling and final verification also affect when the paragraph commits.
  • Pressing Stop captures 640 ms of extra right context, then flushes outstanding work.

These waits overlap. They are not independent constants to add to every caption, and detector timestamps are not word timestamps.

What is inside the model’s inference time?

The live log combines the visual encoder and autoregressive beam search. An earlier detailed local profile measured about 23 ms for the visual encoder and 284 ms for beam search. Those are separate-example measurements, not an exact split of this session’s 289 ms median.

Word generation runs sequentially and becomes more expensive when search takes more steps. This session’s 638 ms maximum is not a bound on longer utterances or repeated-token cases.
