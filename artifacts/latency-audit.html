Waiting that compute timings don’t show

2 s initial bufferThe first inference starts once this much video arrives. It still needs processing afterward.
1 s update intervalA newly captured event waits 0–1 s for the next scheduled update. Slow processing adds queueing and skips intermediate updates.
480 ms future contextThe speaking detector waits for 12 future frames. Its scores also use a trailing 25-frame average and start confirmation.
800 ms confirmed quietClosing a statement requires quiet evidence, future context, then a final recognition pass. Score smoothing can extend this further.

These waits overlap; do not sum them as independent fixed delays. The 6 s rolling history is a lookback window, not a 6 s wait before every result.

Confirmed redundant work: activity inspection and recognition have separate frame/landmark caches. Recognition repeats roughly 153 ms of face detection and landmarks per update. The activity path also spends roughly 188 ms warping FLARE inputs that it discards. These are separate measured medians, not a guaranteed combined saving.

The main recognition pass re-encodes and decodes the full current utterance on every update. Beam search waits for remaining candidates to finish; a short winning phrase does not guarantee a short decode.

Observed delay and measurement limits

Ordinary replay, without profiling probes: two saved-clip scenarios produced their first caption at 3.5–4.1 s after capture began. During recognition updates, the median age of the newest input frame at response receipt was 1.92–1.99 s. This is not word-aligned speech latency.

A constructed speech → quiet replay committed the statement about 5.6 s after the switch. That includes smoothing, scheduling, queueing and a final decode with repetition checking; it is not a measurement of your natural pause boundary.

Laptop camera exposure, browser JPEG encoding, tailnet transit and screen paint are still unmeasured. A browser-side capture-timestamp → response/paint timer would measure these together with server delay, without needing synchronized clocks.

Earlier long-input validation included a 23.1 s decoding outlier on a 60 s constructed clip. That was not typical live latency, but it makes decoder tail latency a separate optimization target.
